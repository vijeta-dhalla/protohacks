Intro 1 of 4: How a breadboard works

A solderless breadboard lets you build circuits without soldering.

  • Many five-hole groups in the centre are connected internally.
  • The centre gap separates the two sides of the board.
  • Long side rails are commonly used for power (5 V) and ground (GND).
  • Components work only when their leads connect to the intended nodes.

Intro 2 of 4: Voltage, current, and Ohm’s law

Voltage is electrical potential difference. Current is the flow of charge. Resistance opposes current. Ohm’s law is V = I × R.

You can rearrange it to I = V / R or R = V / I. Example: 2 V across 1 kilohm gives I = 2/1000 A = 2 mA.

The Arduino measures voltage or digital state; the game compares the measurements with the expected range or behaviour for each challenge.

Intro 3 of 4: How WireSmart works

  1. Read the component information and inspect the target schematic.
  2. Build the circuit on the physical breadboard.
  3. Select Check circuit. The tester reads the assigned Arduino pin.
  4. The program compares the result with the expected behaviour.
  5. If the circuit passes, earn points and repeat or advance.

Intro 4 of 4: Your parts kit

  • Arduino Uno and USB cable
  • Solderless breadboard and jumper wires
  • Red LED and 330-ohm resistor
  • Tactile pushbutton
  • 10-kilohm potentiometer
  • Two 1-kilohm resistors for series/parallel challenges
  • Fixed 10-kilohm reference resistor in the tester

Keep the tester’s 5 V, GND, and sensing leads fixed. Do not connect external voltages to the Arduino analog inputs.
